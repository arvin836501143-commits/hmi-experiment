# Abstract

With the rapid progression of intelligent connected vehicles, the design language of automotive Human-Machine Interfaces (HMI) is undergoing a profound "minimalist" revolution. Numerous emerging electric and smart vehicle brands have chosen to thoroughly eliminate traditional physical instrument clusters and Head-Up Displays (HUDs), highly integrating core driving telemetry, navigation prompts, and infotainment matrices into a unified Central Information Display (CID). While this mono-screen minimalist layout substantially elevates aesthetic sleekness and industrial integration, it simultaneously reconfigures the driver's visual scanning trajectories and cognitive processing pipelines, posing severe human factors and traffic safety challenges. When confronted with high-density, high-conflict visual environments, an individual's information processing efficiency is heavily modulated by their idiosyncratic cognitive style. Positioned within the rigorous context of a "no-instrument, no-HUD" central mono-screen minimalist cockpit, this investigation utilizes the classic Field Dependence-Independence (FDI) cognitive style framework to systematically scrutinize the interactive mechanisms of alternative HMI layouts and drivers' psychological traits on emergency reaction performance and subjective mental workload.

A comprehensive Web-based behavioral experimental platform was custom-engineered to recruit and evaluate 61 experienced drivers. The empirical architecture operationalized a 2 (Cognitive Style: Field Independent [FI] vs. Field Dependent [FD]) × 3 (HMI Layout Grid: SR-dominated, Navigation-dominated, Balanced) mixed-factorial design. Cognitive style served as the between-subjects variable; utilizing the standard Embedded Figures Test (EFT) coupled with a median-split methodology (median = 7.0), the 61 participants were stratified into an FI group (n=32) and an FD group (n=29). The HMI layout configuration was manipulated as the within-subjects variable, comprising: (1) An SR-dominated layout dedicating 75% of screen real estate to 3D virtual environment reconstruction with 25% allocated to marginal navigation feeds; (2) A Navigation-dominated layout assigning 75% of space to a dense 2D planar map with velocity and collision warnings shrunk into extreme text blocks at the perimeter; and (3) A Balanced layout split evenly (50%:50%) mimicking the mainstream Tesla V11 interface. Following complete counterbalancing through a Latin Square design to neutralize sequential learning biases, all participants underwent a rigorous two-stage behavioral appraisal. In Stage 1 (Experiment 1), drivers viewed an 8-second realistic simulated driving video, which abruptly transitioned into a static cockpit hotspot-clicking paradigm to map their exact reaction times (RT) to sudden forward pre-collision alerts (where the lead vehicle flashed vivid crimson). In Stage 2 (Experiment 2), immediately following the behavioral task, participants explicitly documented their multi-dimensional psychological workload via an online NASA-Task Load Index (NASA-TLX).

Inferential statistical analysis of the dataset revealed the following core conclusions:

1. **Interface layout exerts a highly significant main effect on emergency processing performance and cognitive workload within minimalist cockpit HMIs.** Repeated-measures ANOVA demonstrated that the layout main effect reached high significance for both reaction time (*F*(2, 118) = 36.68, *p*_GG < .001, ηp² = 0.383) and corrected RTLX composite workload score (*F*(2, 118) = 47.14, *p*_GG < .001, ηp² = 0.444). Post hoc comparisons (Bonferroni-corrected) revealed that the SR-dominated layout yielded the fastest reaction times (*M* = 1317.4 ms), significantly outperforming both the Balanced layout (*M* = 1528.8 ms, *p* < .001) and the Navigation-dominated layout (*M* = 2254.5 ms, *p* < .001). Cognitive workload followed a consistent trend, with the SR-dominated layout producing the lowest RTLX (*M* = 31.30) and the Navigation-dominated layout the highest (*M* = 55.10). This demonstrates that dedicating 75% of the screen area to high-density 2D electronic maps induces significant processing delays and cognitive load increments for all drivers, regardless of cognitive style.

2. **The moderating effect of cognitive style on reaction time manifests as a consistent directional trend but does not reach statistical significance.** Field Dependent (FD) drivers were consistently slower than Field Independent (FI) drivers across all three layouts (FI overall *M* = 1595.2 ms vs. FD overall *M* = 1816.2 ms), yet the cognitive style main effect did not reach significance (*F*(1, 59) = 1.92, *p* = .171, ηp² = 0.032). Notably, under the Balanced layout condition, the RT difference between FD and FI drivers reached marginal significance (*t* = -1.82, *p* = .074, *d* = -0.47), suggesting that the 50%:50% physical symmetry may exert a marginally beneficial anchoring effect for FD individuals. Exploratory correlational analyses further revealed that EFT continuous scores were marginally and negatively correlated with reaction time under the Navigation-dominated layout (*r* = -0.23, *p* = .079) and the Balanced layout (*r* = -0.24, *p* = .067), indicating that higher field independence is associated with shorter reaction times.

3. **The overall Cognitive Style × Layout interaction does not reach significance, but a significant moderating pattern emerges on the subjective Self-Performance dimension.** The interaction effects were non-significant for both reaction time (*F*(2, 118) = 0.63, *p* = .535) and RTLX composite workload (*F*(2, 118) = 2.09, *p* = .128), indicating that the layout influence pattern is largely consistent across the two cognitive style groups. However, in-depth multidimensional analysis revealed that the Cognitive Style × Layout interaction reached significance on the NASA-TLX "Self-Performance" dimension (*F*(2, 118) = 3.17, *p* = .046, ηp² = 0.051): under the Navigation-dominated layout, FD drivers rated their own task performance significantly lower than FI drivers (FD: *M* = 47.21 vs. FI: *M* = 38.03), indicating that FD individuals subjectively experienced stronger performance frustration in high-noise interfaces. Additionally, EFT continuous scores were significantly and positively correlated with RTLX total scores under the SR-dominated layout (*r* = +0.48, *p* < .001) and the Balanced layout (*r* = +0.44, *p* < .001), suggesting that individuals with higher field independence reported greater subjective cognitive load under these layouts, potentially reflecting higher meta-cognitive sensitivity among FI individuals.

Synthesizing these empirical findings through the dual lenses of automotive human factors and inclusive design, this study proposes HMI optimization recommendations centered on the layout main effect. The SR-dominated layout demonstrated the best overall performance across both objective performance and subjective workload dimensions, and is recommended as the default configuration for minimalist mono-screen cockpits. This research not only enriches the empirical body of engineering psychology in the domain of intelligent vehicle interaction but also provides scientific psychological evidence for driving safety optimization in the context of the global automotive industry's migration toward mono-screen minimalism.
